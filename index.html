Know your rights under the Fair Credit Reporting Act

This app gives general information, not legal advice. For a complicated situation, talk to a consumer attorney or a nonprofit credit counselor.

Your basic rights

  • Free reports. You can get your credit reports from all three bureaus for free at AnnualCreditReport.com, the only official site.
  • Right to dispute (§ 611, 15 U.S.C. § 1681i). If a bureau's report has something incomplete or wrong, you can dispute it. The bureau must investigate, usually within 30 days (45 days if you send more information during the 30 days, or if the dispute follows your free annual report).
  • Results in writing. The bureau must send you the results within 5 business days after it finishes, and a free updated report if anything changed.
  • Unverifiable items come off. If the information can't be verified, it must be corrected or deleted.
  • Ask how it was verified (§ 611(a)(7)). If the bureau says an item is verified, you can ask for a description of how it checked, including the name, address and phone number of the company it contacted. It must answer within 15 days of your request.
  • Dispute the creditor directly (§ 623, 15 U.S.C. § 1681s-2). You can also dispute with the company that reported the item. It must investigate and respond, normally within 30 days.
  • Add a statement. If the dispute doesn't resolve it, you can ask the bureau to add a short statement to your file.
  • Old negative items expire (§ 605). Most negative information can't be reported after 7 years. Chapter 7 bankruptcies can stay 10 years.
  • Identity theft block (§ 605B, 15 U.S.C. § 1681c-2). If an account came from identity theft, the bureau must block it within 4 business days after getting your identity theft report, proof of identity, and a letter naming the items.
  • You can sue. If a bureau or creditor willfully or negligently breaks the FCRA, you may be able to recover damages and attorney's fees.

A simple process

  1. Pull all three reports and mark every error.
  2. Gather proof: statements, payment confirmations, letters, ID.
  3. Add each error in the Disputes tab, then print a § 611 letter for each bureau that shows it. Also send a § 623 letter to the creditor if you like.
  4. Mail by certified mail, return receipt requested. Record the date you mailed it.
  5. Watch the deadline. If the bureau says "verified," send the follow-up letter asking how.
  6. If you still can't get it fixed, file a complaint with the CFPB.

Common negative items and what to check

Late payments
Stay 7 years from the late payment. Compare each month's 30/60/90-day code with your bank records. A payment made during an agreed forbearance or deferment, or made on time but posted late, can be disputed. If the late was accurate, you can still ask the creditor for a "goodwill" removal, but they don't have to agree.
Charge-offs
The creditor wrote the debt off as a loss, but you may still owe it. It stays 7 years from the date of first delinquency (plus 180 days), and that date can't be moved forward when the debt is sold. If the account was sold to a collector, the original creditor should show a $0 balance. If you paid or settled it, it should say so.
Repossessions
Stay 7 years from the first missed payment. After the sale, the lender must credit what the vehicle sold for. Check that the deficiency balance matches the sale notice you received, and that a voluntary surrender isn't reported as an involuntary repo.
Collections
Keep the original debt's 7-year clock. Watch for the same debt listed by more than one collector, or a newer delinquency date than the original account. Under the Fair Debt Collection Practices Act you can also ask the collector to validate the debt within 30 days of their first notice.
Foreclosures
Stay 7 years. A short sale or deed in lieu shouldn't be labeled a foreclosure, and the mortgage shouldn't keep showing a balance afterwards.
Bankruptcy
Chapter 7 can stay 10 years from filing, Chapter 13 usually 7. Every debt discharged in it should show $0 and "included in bankruptcy," not past due.
Judgments and tax liens
Equifax, Experian and TransUnion stopped including civil judgments and tax liens in credit reports in 2017–2018. If one shows up, dispute it. For federal tax liens, see IRS: Understanding a federal tax lien and how to get a lien released or withdrawn.
Hard inquiries
Stay 2 years and only matter for about one. Dispute any you didn't authorize, and check for identity theft if you see several you don't recognize.

    Identity theft and the IRS

    • Report identity theft and get your official Identity Theft Report at IdentityTheft.gov (FTC). You'll need this report for a § 605B block.
    • If someone used your Social Security number to file a tax return, or the IRS tells you about a return you didn't file, follow the IRS steps at IRS Identity Theft Central. You may need Form 14039, Identity Theft Affidavit.
    • Get an IRS Identity Protection PIN to stop others from filing a return in your name.
    • To prove income or tax filing for a lender, you can get a free transcript from IRS Get Transcript.

    Watch out for scams

    No one can legally remove accurate, timely negative information. Under the Credit Repair Organizations Act, a credit repair company can't charge you before it finishes the work it promised. Everything this app does, you can do yourself for free.
